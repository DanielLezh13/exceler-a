## Accuracy decisions

- The guide's haplorrhine blanks name the monkey/ape branches. Tarsiers remain a third branch, not omitted or classified as strepsirrhines.
- The general haplorrhine orbital rule is qualified for partial tarsier closure. Nocturnality, grooming claws, and unfused mandibles are not treated as universal suborder classifiers.
- A bar is the ring; closure adds a back wall. A closed orbit still has a bar.
- Most New World monkeys use 2.1.3.3; many marmosets/tamarins use 2.1.3.2. Catarrhines use 2.1.2.3. Formula order and per-quadrant meaning are explicitly taught.
- Not all platyrrhines have prehensile tails. Catarrhines lack prehensile tails, not all tails. Apes lack external tails.
- Catarrhine nostrils are described as narrow/closely spaced/downward, resolving the contradictory phrase “broad forward-facing” in Class 9 slide 6.
- Natural hybridization does not imply every hybrid is sterile. Wild yellow/anubis baboons provide a researched natural example; a captive liger supplies the zoo example. See [Fogel et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8635413/).
- Hair, mammary glands, and three middle-ear bones are the core mammal traits. Mammals already existed before the end-Cretaceous extinction. No requirement to memorize the slide's unsupported claim that all modern orders existed by 75 mya. See [OpenStax Mammals](https://openstax.org/books/biology/pages/29-6-mammals).
- Mutation is random with respect to usefulness; drift concerns chance sampling. Selection/drift can operate together. The guide's “completely random” contrast is explained rather than made into a false claim that only drift has randomness.
- Similarity between different species does not alone prove analogy. No requirement to infer independent tail evolution from species difference alone.
- Group-level traits do not uniquely establish a continent or exact species. Identification exercises deliberately test that limit.
- Do not reproduce slide overgeneralizations about every ape brachiating, all primate males dominating females, all hybrids being sterile, all nonhuman learning excluding teaching, or every food adaptation determining all group behavior.

Family-name lists, exact changing species counts, researcher names, extinct lemur weights, and conservation-status trivia are not required merely because they appear in slides. Functional hypotheses and group-specific feeding details are supporting slide practice; the guide's comparisons and mechanisms remain the priority. Fossil-human content is not added.
